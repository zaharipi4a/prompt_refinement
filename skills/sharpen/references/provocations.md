# Provocations

One per sharpen round, at most. Choose the one most likely to change the user's mind or reveal
something they haven't said. Adapt the wording to the request; don't paste it word for word.

## Find the real goal
- "If this works perfectly, what's different a month from now?"
- "Is the [thing] the goal, or a means to something else? What is that?"
- "Who is this really for: the user, you, or someone you want to impress?"

## Pre-mortem
- "Imagine you look at the finished result and feel let down. What's the most likely reason?"
- "What would make this feel generic?"
- "Where have you seen this done badly, and what made it bad?"

## Sharpen taste
- "Name one thing (a site, a film, an app, a book cover) that has the feeling you want."
- "What's the opposite of what you want? Being specific about what you dislike helps a lot."
- "If it could only do one thing really well, which thing?"

## Cut scope
- "What is the smallest version that would still be worth showing someone?"
- "Which part are you most excited about? Should we start there?"
- "What could we leave out completely without anyone noticing?"

## Question the assumption
- "You asked for X. Would Y solve the same problem with half the work?"
- "What would you do if [the main constraint] disappeared?"
- "Is this a one-off or something you'll repeat? That changes how we should build it."

## Make it distinctive
- "What would make someone remember this a week later?"
- "What's the obvious way to do this, and do we want to be obvious here?"
- "What do you know about this audience that a competitor doesn't?"
